SpiderIndia

A community science initiative to map the spiders of the Indian subcontinent, run with and by the people who go out and find them.

Spiders are the richest chelicerate lineage by a wide margin, and in South Asia they are also among the least recorded. Closing that gap is not something a lab can do on its own.

SpiderIndia began in 2005 as a mailing list started by Vijay Barve, where naturalists helped each other identify spiders from field photographs. Membership was around 400 by 2011, when the community moved to Facebook. A group on the India Biodiversity Portal followed in 2012, and later an iNaturalist project to capture Indian araneae records systematically in one place. Today more than 8,000 people take part.

Annual meets began in Kolkata in 2016 and have run since, moving each year to a different landscape. Spider Week, held each August, is a concentrated recording push: the 2020 edition logged over 5,500 observations from 225 participants, and 2021 passed 5,000 again.

How to take part

Record what you find

Photograph spiders wherever you are and post them to the SpiderIndia project on iNaturalist. Location and date matter more than a perfect picture.

Ask for an identification

The Facebook group is where most identification happens, with experienced arachnologists answering.

Come to a meet

Meets are held annually, in the field, and are open to anyone. Write to spiderindia.in@gmail.com for the next one.
